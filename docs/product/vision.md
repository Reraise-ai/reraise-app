# Vision

> The model is replaceable. Your personal world is not.

This page states what The Worldbuilder is and why it exists. Part 1 renders the substance of
the owner's product definition (21 sections, condensed). Part 2 states the concept vision
this repository builds, including the decisions that refine or supersede details of Part 1.
Where the two differ, Part 2 is the binding canon.

## Part 1: Product definition

### 1. Starting point

The starting point is not that people lack AI. Technically capable people increasingly build
their own private AI and multi-agent systems: chat assistants, local models, cloud desktops,
wikis, second brains, databases, automations, browsers, files, their own agents. The question
is no longer "where do I get an AI?" but: **how do I bring my own world into my AI system so
that it can actually work from my knowledge, my experience, my premises and my decision
logic?**

Personal context is scattered across chats, files, notes, wikis, heads and implicit
experience. Most AI systems hang on a handful of large models, with their training data,
system limits and gatekeepers. The Worldbuilder puts a layer of its own between person and
model. The central idea: the model is replaceable; the person's world is not.

### 2. What it is

It is not a chatbot, not a wiki, not a classic second brain, not a plain knowledge database
and not a fine-tuning system. It is a personal (later also communal) knowledge, world and
decision infrastructure for AI systems.

A person models their world in it: what they know and have experienced, which sources they
use, which statements they consider reliable, what others claim, what they derive
themselves, which relations hold between things, which premises drive their decisions, and
which decisions and experiences result. The world stays with the user. The language model
becomes an exchangeable execution layer.

### 3. The card

The card is the basic unit: not a text document, but a structured element of a world. It can
represent a person, organisation, actor, event, claim, source, experience, relation,
assumption, personal rating, decision, decision rule, business field, topic or any other
subject. It can carry source, origin, rating, visibility, affiliation, relations and context.

The essential separation:

- **Evidenced**: something can be traced to a source or an original.
- **Said**: someone made a particular statement.
- **Believed / rated**: the user holds something to be right or relevant.

These categories must never be merged into an artificial "central truth". A personal
conclusion such as "I consider this regional property market excellent" need not be
objectively proven. It may be a well-founded conclusion from own experience, contacts and
results, and it has high value for the owner's own decision process.

### 4. The world

A world is the totality of cards, relations, experiences, premises and decision logic that
matter to a user in a given personal or topical context. It is not a data set but a modelled
slice of reality from a particular perspective: a world of property deals in one region, of
geopolitics, of one's company, of family and relationships, of personal finances.

A card can appear in several worlds. It is not copied; it is used in context, and the same
card may mean something different in each.

### 5. Example: knowledge becomes economically usable

Take a world about property deals in one region. It holds public facts, but also own
experience, market observations, personal contacts, successful and failed deals, personal
assessments, concrete properties, local actors, legal and tax information and own business
strategies. A card can say: "I consider this market attractive because I made these
experiences, built these contacts and saw these opportunities."

Another person has another world and other conclusions. When two people interact, cards or
parts of their worlds can be related or deliberately exchanged, and something real can
result: cooperation, a deal, a contact, the sale of a flat. The economic value of a card
lies in personal experience, provenance, assessment, classification and practical usability.

### 6. The next level: decisions

Not "more knowledge" but capturing **decisions**. Knowledge: "I know this market." Decision
knowledge: "Under these conditions I consider this deal attractive and would act
accordingly."

A decision card or rule holds: situation, trigger, relevant conditions, known facts,
personal priorities, preferred action, alternatives, exclusion conditions, rationale, the
knowledge cards used, and the later outcome. What is stored is not only what the user
knows, but gradually how they decide.

The goal is not immediate training or fine-tuning. First, decision knowledge is structured
explicitly and retrieved on demand. The AI then asks not only "what does my owner know about
this topic?" but "which of their assumptions, experiences and decision rules are relevant to
this situation, and how would they probably rate them?"

### 7. The board is the decisive UX

The board is not a pretty visualisation of the database. It is the central interface for
thinking, analysing and deciding, and one of the most important points of the project. The
database stores cards and relations, an API supplies context, a model produces answers, but
the human needs a surface on which to grasp the world as a **situation** and work with it.
The board is where individual cards become a concrete situation.

### 8. The board as playing field

Take geopolitics. A personal premise such as "geopolitics beats markets" is not plain
information but a strategic assumption of the personal world. Information arrives from many
sources: long podcasts, analysts, original sources, media reports, historical data, own
observations, derivations, statements by actors.

The question is not only "what do these pieces of information say?" but "what is happening
on the field?" Players appear on the board (for example a handful of states and alliances),
with their interests, capabilities, dependencies, relations, past moves, public statements,
observed actions, contradictions between statement and behaviour, and possible next moves.
Cards supply the information; the board makes the game situation visible.

The user reads: what is player X trying to do? Why does player Y make exactly this move?
Which reaction is likely? Which of my assumptions does this support, which does it weaken,
and which new piece of information does not fit my model at all? Game theory, strategy and
the 36 Stratagems are additional thinking tools. The board is not only a display but a
cognitive workspace for strategic thinking.

### 9. The board connects knowledge and decision

The loop: **experience → card → world → board/situation → analysis → decision → action →
outcome → new experience.** A decision does not stand isolated at the end of a report; it
acts back on the world. After a successful decision, the experience becomes relevant again
later. If a source repeatedly proved right, its rating rises. If an assumption repeatedly
fails, its rating falls. If the relation between actors changes, future boards are
influenced. The world does not only grow, it becomes more experienced.

### 10. Source rating and analytics

Not only "analyst X said this", but "how often was analyst X later right?" The system can
determine which statements were verifiable, how often they held, how often they were wrong,
in which topics the source is good, under which conditions forecasts are reliable, how often
the source agrees with the own world, how often it contradicts it, and how valuable that
contradiction turned out to be in hindsight.

The properties are kept apart: source A has a high hit rate but strong ideological
deviation; B has high hit rate and high agreement; C is rarely right but very useful as a
counter-perspective. There is **no simple credibility score**. The dimensions are hit
accuracy, reliability, topic competence, agreement with the own world, deviation from the
own world, quality of the sources used, and historical performance.

### 11. Own experience is evidence too, of a different kind

"A well-known thinker says method X works" is not the same as "I applied method X five times
in my family and it worked in four cases". The second is no scientific proof of a universal
truth, but valuable personal experience that over time becomes personal decision knowledge:
"under these conditions this approach repeatedly worked for this person or situation". It is
usable for family, psychology, personal development, learning and relationships. Well-known
thinkers can appear as sources, thinkers or reference systems, but their statements do not
automatically become truth; the user decides their standing.

### 12. The personal world is not an objective world

There is no built-in authority called "the system's official truth". Perspectives differ
through basic assumptions, thinkers, experiences, interests, cultural imprint, strategies,
personal relationships and economic experience. The Worldbuilder makes the differences
visible and machine-readable. A world may contain contradictions; consensus is never
forced.

### 13. The platform: from one private world to many worlds

The second big leap. Every user has a world of their own, private by default. Selected cards
are shared deliberately. Many independent private knowledge systems become connectable.
Take a card about a contested historical event: one person holds technical aspects, one
political consequences, one financing and actors, one a source that refutes a particular
claim, one holds no card at all. Branches emerge: a richer structure than any individual
could build.

### 14. Many identical cards are not independent confirmation

10,000 people adopting the same agency report is not 10,000 independent confirmations.
Provenance: source A is the original report; B cites A; analyst C cites B; media report D is
based on C. "Four cards exist, but possibly there is only one independent origin of
information." The question is: how independent is the information from each other?

### 15. The SETI idea

An individual covers a limited slice. Many independent participants cover a larger search
space: finding different sources, gaining experience, discovering connections, noting
contradictions, flagging errors, creating new cards, completing existing ones and offering
opposing hypotheses. This is not "the more believe it, the truer". The benefit: the more
independent people contribute different information, sources, counter-evidence and
experience, the less any single instance can control which knowledge is visible, checkable
and comparable. This is the anti-gatekeeper effect.

### 16. Gatekeepers lose power through decentralisation

Gatekeepers are search engines, media, platforms, data providers, AI providers and large
model operators. With The Worldbuilder the user owns their world, uses it across different
models, and others voluntarily share parts of theirs. Not one central truth, but many
connected, traceable perspectives.

### 17. The central AI interface

Model-independent. The user should not re-enter the whole world into a model each time. An
agent asks: "which information and decision rules are relevant to this situation?" The
pipeline: **request → active world → relevant cards → relevant relations → relevant
decisions → sources and provenance → context package → model.** The personal world stays
outside the model. It is usable with any commercial model, a local model, a specialised
model or the user's own agent.

### 18. The Worldbuilder and multi-agent systems

It does not replace a multi-agent system; it supplies the missing layer: persistent personal
knowledge and persistent decision logic. Several agents and models use the same world. The
architecture is person → own world → Worldbuilder → agents, models and tools, not person →
one AI provider → everything else.

### 19. The four central layers

1. **Personal world**: cards, sources, experiences, relations, assumptions, ratings.
2. **Decision world**: decisions, rules, priorities, action patterns, outcomes, feedback.
3. **Board / UX**: the surface on which a person sees a concrete situation, assembles cards,
   looks at actors and relations, analyses scenarios and prepares decisions.
4. **Platform**: connection of several worlds, deliberate card exchange, provenance,
   independent contributions, communal verification, model-independent access.

Around them: infrastructure, API, database, analytics, authentication, integrations.

### 20. The overall picture

My life and my world → knowledge, experience, decisions → **cards** → relations and sources
→ **world** → a concrete situation or topic → **board** (actors, evidence, assumptions) →
analysis and AI → **decision** → action → outcome → new experience, flowing back.

In parallel, the platform: private world A shares released cards into a common knowledge
graph; private world B does the same. Origin, independence, counter-evidence, ratings and
practical experience are preserved throughout.

### 21. The overarching idea

Not "my AI knows my files" but "my AI knows my world". Later: "my AI increasingly
understands how I assess situations and make decisions". On the platform: "my world can
interact with other worlds without a single gatekeeper deciding what is visible or
relevant". A personal, persistent, exchangeable knowledge and decision layer, with
replaceable language models.

The closing thought: the decisive point for further development is not first the database,
but **what the human sees and does on the board at the moment their world becomes a concrete
decision situation.** That is the UX to which data model, AI interface and platform logic
should then align.

## Part 2: Concept vision of this repository

This repository builds the first, local-first slice of that vision, plus the open interfaces
that let it sit on top of any person's existing knowledge system. The decisions below are
binding and each is (or will be) recorded as an [ADR](../adr/README.md).

### What we build

- **A layer between a person and any model or agent.** Not a chatbot, wiki or second brain.
  Your world stays on your device, models and agents come and go.
- **A universal adapter architecture.** The product's domain model is canonical. Any
  knowledge store (a Markdown vault, a second brain, another agent system's wiki, a legal
  database, a paid news service) is reached through adapters. Cards can be referenced by URI
  with their provenance kept.
- **The board as the central UX.** A guiding question, seats (players), cards, edges,
  evidence, a rules panel and a decision card. See [Board UX](./board-ux.md).
- **Humans and agents share the board.** An agent's move is a proposal with an author. A
  human accepts or rejects it. Nothing an agent proposed counts anywhere until a human has
  accepted it.

### What we deliberately do not build

- **No card intake.** Cards are created in the user's own AI, wiki or pipeline. We read and
  reference them through adapters. There is no ingestion of podcasts or articles. The inbox
  only shows "new findings from connected sources", referenced with provenance and pinned on
  demand.
- **No probability barometer and no verdict.** A meter such as "hold 69 : 31" reads as the
  system's judgement. In its place the Rules panel lists which of the user's own rules apply
  right now (fired or not fired, with the cards that triggered them), with no percentage.
- **No economy layer.** Monetisation, card exchange economics, gamification and crypto are
  out of scope. The data model keeps globally unique card IDs, a content hash, a reserved
  `level` field for future card tiers and a reserved `signature` field, and builds nothing
  on them.
- **No central truth and no consensus requirement.** Contradictions stay visible.

### Canon in short

| Principle | In practice |
| --- | --- |
| Layers never merge | `layer` is `evidenced`, `said` or `experience`. "Believed" is not a layer but a private **stance** overlay per user per card. See [why layers never merge](../explanation/layers-never-merge.md). |
| Provenance is mandatory | Evidenced and said cards need a source or origin system; experience needs an author. Counting origins, not copies. See [provenance and independence](../explanation/provenance-and-independence.md). |
| Files are the truth | Markdown with YAML front matter is the storage canon. Any database or index is derived and rebuildable. History is archived versions, not an event log. |
| Privacy by default | A disclosure gate decides per card and per target (screen, local model, cloud model, demo). Default is deny. A cloud transfer needs per-transfer confirmation. Stances never leave the device. |
| Two thinking levels | Level 1 is a fast, typed answer ("`<engine>` estimates 0.82 for X"), never a verdict, escalatable to Level 2, the board. |
| Multi-dimensional source profile | Hit accuracy, reliability, topic competence per topic, agreement, deviation, source quality, historical performance and counter-perspective value, each with a sample size. Never one score. |
| Training is opt-in | A JSONL export of decision traces, only for cards with `training: true`. Facts stay retrievable cards; what may be trained is the decision style. |
| The model is replaceable | See [why the LLM is replaceable](../explanation/llm-is-replaceable.md). |

### The loop

experience → card → world → board → analysis → decision → action → outcome → new
experience. The decision card's outcome flows back as experience and into source statistics.
Milestone M1 proves the first thin end-to-end path; the loop closes in the M2+ slices. See
the [roadmap](../roadmap.md).
