# Board UX

The board is the heart of The Worldbuilder. It is where a personal world turns into a
concrete **Lage** (situation) around one guiding question, and the situation into a
decision. This page describes what a person sees and does. It is the UX specification only:
architecture is derived separately (see [Architecture](../architecture/index.md)) and the
two are linked, not merged.

This page is an English adaptation of concept v1, updated to the owner's later decisions
(see [concept v1 notes](./concept-v1-notes.md)). All names, numbers and events in examples
are invented; see [Scenarios](./scenarios.md).

::: warning Status legend
Nothing on this page is a shipped feature yet. M0 delivers contracts only. Every capability
is therefore labelled:

- **planned (M1)**: part of the walking skeleton, one Playwright end-to-end scenario.
- **planned (M2+)**: a later vertical slice. See the [roadmap](../roadmap.md).

The [verification table](../verification.md) records which claims are backed by tests.
:::

## Prototype v1 (frozen design study)

[`prototype/board.html`](https://github.com/Reraise-ai/reraise-app/blob/main/prototype/board.html)
is a frozen, clickable design study from concept v1. It is kept as a reference for look and
flow and is not maintained. It deviates from the current canon in these places, and the
canon on this page wins:

| Prototype v1 shows | Current canon |
| --- | --- |
| A premise barometer counting support and weakening | No barometer. A [Rules panel](#rules-panel) lists fired and not fired rules with their triggering cards, no percentage. |
| An ingest triage that extracts statements from podcasts and articles | No ingestion. The [inbox](#inbox-new-findings-from-connected-sources) shows new findings from connected sources, referenced with provenance and pinned on demand. |
| Four epistemic layers including "believed" | Three layers. "Believed" is a private [stance overlay](#layers-and-the-stance-overlay). |
| Ghost cards generated by an AI | [Proposals with an author](#seats-and-agent-proposals) from humans and agents alike. |
| Data kept in the page | Files are the truth. Persistence is an adapter concern (planned M1). |

## 1. Design principles

Each principle is phrased as a check for design decisions and for coding agents.

1. **Never merge layers.** A card always shows whether it is evidenced, said or experience.
   No view, counter or summary adds layers together without a breakdown. Check: every number
   on the board can be broken down into its layers by one click.
2. **Agents propose, humans decide.** Every agent move is a proposal with an author. Only a
   human accepting it makes it real. Check: there is no code path in which an agent creates
   an accepted card, edge or placement. An agent can never set a stance.
3. **Every board has a guiding question.** A board without one is an error state, not an
   empty state. The question is always visible at the top.
4. **Contradiction is valuable, not an error.** Contradictions (between statements, between
   statement and action, between a source and me) are highlighted and never resolved
   automatically. There is no "fix conflict" button.
5. **Premises are visible and attackable.** The premises of the world stay on screen, and
   the board shows what supports and what weakens them.
6. **What does not fit stays visible.** Information that does not fit the model goes to the
   **foreign-body tray** (German: Fremdkörper). It is neither discarded nor forced into
   place.
7. **Origin before volume.** Counters show origins, not cards: "4 cards · 1 independent
   origin". The number of cards is never an argument.
8. **No verdicts.** The board never shows a score that reads as the system's judgement. It
   shows which of your own rules apply and which cards triggered them.
9. **Phases guide, they do not force.** The human moves freely between phases. The system
   hints, never blocks.
10. **One step, one gesture.** Frequent actions are one-click or keyboard actions. Typing is
    needed only where the human has to think (rationale, stance).
11. **Show uncertainty, never smooth it.** Missing data shows "too little data (n=3)", not a
    number. Sources get profiles, not stars.
12. **A lens, not a copy.** The same cards appear in every lens. Positions are layout and
    change no content.
13. **Everything is traceable.** Every card, edge, proposal and stance change has an origin
    (who, when, with what) and a visible history (archived versions, not an event log).
14. **Private stays private.** A stance never leaves the device. Anything that may leave
    passes the [disclosure gate](#disclosure-gate-and-cloud-warning).

## 2. Visual grammar

### Layers and the stance overlay

Status: planned (M1).

| Layer | Frame | Marker | Required on the card |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Evidenced** | solid, double line | seal icon, top left | source link and location |
| **Said** | single, with a speech-bubble corner | avatar and name of the speaker | speaker, location, date |
| **Experience** | single, border with tally dots | counter such as "4 of 5" and outcome dots | number of attempts, outcomes, author |
| **Proposal** | dashed, reduced opacity | author badge (human or `agent:<name>`) | author, kind, payload |

**Believed is not a layer.** It is a *stance*: the user's own position on a card, stored per
user per card as `position` (believed, rejected, undecided), `weight`, `note`, `updatedAt`,
with history kept. A stance appears as a strip under the card in the user's own accent
colour. An evidenced or said card can at once be "said by X" and "believed by me at weight
0.7", and both are visible. A card with no stance has no strip; the strip is never filled
with a default. Stances are private and never leave the device. A legacy "believed" card
from another system is read as a said card authored by the user plus a believed stance.

### Edges on the canvas

Status: planned (M1) for supports; the rest planned (M2+).

| Edge kind | Line | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `supports` | solid green, arrow | the source card supports the target |
| `refutes` | solid red, double arrow | the source card refutes the target |
| `represents` | thin, labelled | an actor or speaker stands for a position |
| `cites` | thin grey, dotted arrow towards the origin | provenance chain |
| `based_on` | thin grey arrow | derivation or dependence on a basis |
| `depends_on` | dashed arrow towards the dependent | an actor's dependency |
| `relation` | line weight = weight | alliance, conflict or other typed relation |
| `unspecified` | thin neutral | legacy or not yet classified |

Every edge records who drew it (`by`: `human` or `agent:<name>`). A proposed edge is dashed
until a human accepts it. A statement-versus-action contradiction is drawn with a lightning
mark and a contradiction between two cards with a scales mark; neither is ever auto-resolved.

### Badges and counters

| Badge | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `4 cards · 1 origin` | provenance: number of cards and number of independent origin roots |
| `N findings · M origins` | the source pass on a card, see [source pass](#source-pass) |
| Lightning | statement-versus-action contradiction |
| Scales | contradiction between cards (`refutes`) |
| Clock | forecast open, with resolution date |
| Check / cross / half | forecast came true / did not / partly |
| Gear | a rule fired on this situation |

## 3. Screen layout

Status: planned (M1) for the core regions, the rest planned (M2+).

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| WORLD: Geopolitics [v]   as of: now [v]   [what-if: off]      seats: you, 1 agent |
| PREMISES: [Interests beat rhetoric]  [+2 more]                                    |
| GUIDING QUESTION: Does the Hormuz situation escalate within three months?         |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| PHASES: Ask > Populate > Read > Check > Decide > Follow up       LENS: Field ...  |
+------------------------+-----------------------------------+---------------------+
| WHAT YOUR WORLD KNOWS  | CANVAS                            | CARD DETAIL         |
| [World] [Inbox (3)]    |   [Seat A] <== conflict ==> [B]   | layer, source,      |
| ---------------------- |       |                           | stance, edges,      |
| Proposals (pending)    |   [Seat C]                        | provenance, history |
| - from agent:scout     |                                   | ------------------- |
| - from you             |  FOREIGN-BODY TRAY  [..] [..]     | THINKING            |
| ---------------------- |  time slider ----o-------------   | [Level 1 answer]    |
| Inbox: new findings    |                                   | [Escalate to board] |
+------------------------+-----------------------------------+---------------------+
| RULES: 2 fired, 3 not fired | contradictions 2 | open forecasts 1 | proposals 2   |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

| Region | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Header | world picker, "as of" time, what-if toggle, seats at the table |
| Premises bar | the world's premises as chips. A click shows the supporting and weakening cards. No gauge. |
| Guiding question | the board's question, editable. Each change archives the previous version. |
| Phase bar | the six phases (below) and the lens switcher |
| Left sidebar | **What your world knows** (proposals ranked by relevance to the question) and **Inbox** (new findings from connected sources) |
| Canvas | cards and edges in the active lens, with the foreign-body tray at the bottom |
| Right sidebar | **Card detail** and **Thinking** (Level 1 answers, escalation) |
| Status line | Rules panel summary, contradictions, open forecasts, pending proposals |

Interaction rules: selecting a card fills the detail pane in fixed order (type and layer,
content, source, my stance, edges, provenance, history). Pinning an existing card places a
reference, never a copy. Dropping a card into the tray marks it `parked`, not deleted.

## 4. The six phases

Phases are a visible work flow. The human may jump freely.

| Phase | What the human does | What the system does | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Ask | writes the guiding question, picks the world, lists options | checks that a question exists; may suggest sharpenings as proposals | planned (M1) |
| 2. Populate | pins existing cards, reviews proposals, looks at the inbox of new findings | ranks candidate cards by relevance; shows findings with provenance | planned (M1) for pinning; planned (M2+) for the inbox |
| 3. Read | fills in seats (interests, capabilities, dependencies, moves, each with a source), draws edges, sets stances | shows provenance counters; flags statement-versus-action pairs as proposals | planned (M1) for edges; planned (M2+) for seat fields |
| 4. Check | tests premises, reviews the Rules panel, empties the foreign-body tray, uses thinking tools | evaluates rules (fired or not fired with triggering cards) | planned (M2+) |
| 5. Decide | fills the decision card, picks an option, records a forecast | offers a Level 1 answer if asked; checks the card is complete (hints, no blocking) | planned (M2+) |
| 6. Follow up | records the outcome at the review date | updates source statistics; proposes an experience card | planned (M2+) |

## 5. Lenses

All lenses show the same cards and edges and differ only in spatial grammar. Status: the
free canvas is planned (M1); the other lenses are planned (M2+).

- **Playing field.** Seats as zones with a player card (interests, capabilities,
  dependencies, moves). Lines show alliance, conflict, dependency. Statements sit at the
  zone edge, contradictions carry a lightning mark.
- **Timeline.** Two tracks: statements above, actions below, filterable per seat. A
  statement-versus-action pair is a red arc. A "now" line and a review line mark the horizon.
- **Evidence wall.** One premise or option at the centre, supporting cards on one side and
  refuting cards on the other, grouped by layer, evidenced first. Cards sharing an origin
  root collapse into an **origin stack** ("4 cards · 1 origin"). Below it, raw counts per
  layer, never a verdict.
- **Decision.** One column per option with linked pro and contra cards and their layer. Above
  them the rules that fired. Below them the decision card form.
- **Free canvas.** No structure. Cards are freely placed and edges are lines. The fallback
  when no lens fits.

## 6. Rules panel

Status: planned (M2+). It replaces the probability barometer of concept v1.

The owner rejected a meter such as "hold 69 : 31" because it reads as the system's own
judgement and merges layers. The Rules panel answers a different question: **which of my
rules apply right now?**

- A **rule** is a card of type decision rule: `id`, `when` (conditions referencing cards or
  tags), `then` (the preferred action), `unless` (exclusions) and notes.
- Evaluating the rules against the current Lage yields a **list of rules**, each marked
  **fired** or **not fired**, with the ids of the **cards that triggered it** and, for not
  fired rules, which condition failed.
- There is **no percentage and no ranking**. Rules come from the user's rule cards, never
  from code. Retiring a rule keeps its history.
- A rule that fired is a hint, not a decision. The decision stays with the human.
- Only accepted cards count. A card that an agent merely proposed never triggers a rule.

```
RULES that apply to this situation
  [fired]     R1  "Do not act on the first day of an escalation headline"
                  triggered by: card 01J... (event), card 01J... (statement)
  [not fired] R3  "Hedge only if exposure exceeds the stated limit"
                  condition not met: no card states current exposure
```

## 7. Inbox: new findings from connected sources

Status: planned (M2+). Nothing is ingested.

The Worldbuilder does not turn podcasts or articles into cards. Cards are created in the
user's own system. A **source connector** can report *new findings* from a connected source
(a vault, a wiki, a paid news service, a legal database, another agent's notes). The inbox
lists them.

- A finding is shown **by reference**, with provenance: system, identifier or URI, date.
- Nothing in the inbox is world knowledge until the human **pins** it. Pinning places a
  reference to the card in the user's own store and keeps its original provenance. It does
  not copy and does not rewrite the origin.
- Actions per finding: pin, dismiss (remembered, so it is not shown again), pin as
  foreign body, open the origin.
- The inbox shows the **source pass** for each finding so that ten reports of one origin are
  not mistaken for ten confirmations.

## 8. Seats and agent proposals

Status: planned (M1) for the proposal round trip; live updates planned (M2+).

The board is "looking at the chessboard together". A **seat** is a participant at the board:
`{ name, kind: human | agent }`. Humans and the user's own agents sit at the same board.

An agent move is never a write. It is a **proposal**:

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `id` | proposal id |
| `author` | `agent:<name>` (or a human seat) |
| `kind` | `place_card`, `move_card`, `add_edge`, `create_card`, `comment` |
| `payload` | the content of the move |
| `status` | `pending`, `accepted`, `rejected` |
| `decidedBy`, `decidedAt` | who decided and when (human only) |

- Pending proposals are drawn dashed with the author's name. A human **accepts** or
  **rejects** each. Accepting writes through the normal store path with the author kept.
- Nothing pending counts anywhere: not in rules, not in statistics, not in context packages
  as accepted fact.
- The agent-facing MCP tools can only create and list proposals. The human-only endpoints
  accept and reject.
- A "who sits at the table" view shows connected seats and what each last read or proposed.

## 9. Thinking levels

Status: planned (M2+).

| Level | Name | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Fast | A typed, schema-bound answer in milliseconds to seconds: `{engine, options enum, choice, confidence, inputs[]}` from the active world's rules and cards. Engines: the local rules engine, a local model, and a connector to an external decision service (stub, always behind the disclosure gate). |
| 2 | Deliberate | The board: seats, evidence, what-if, agents brainstorming, the decision card. |

- A Level 1 answer is shown as **"`<engine>` estimates 0.82 for X"**, with its inputs. It is
  never shown as the system's verdict.
- Any Level 1 answer can be **escalated** to a Level 2 board: a new Lage seeded with the
  question, options and the answer's input cards.
- Verbal probabilities use the ICD 203 words of estimative probability (almost no chance
  1–5 %, very unlikely 5–20, unlikely 20–45, roughly even chance 45–55, likely 55–80, very
  likely 80–95, almost certain 95–99). The original phrase and the mapped band are both
  stored, and the UI shows words.

## 10. Disclosure gate and cloud warning

Status: planned (M1) for the gate blocking a private card for the cloud target.

Before anything leaves the screen, the gate decides per card and per target
(`screen`, `local_model`, `cloud_model`, `demo`): **allow** or **deny**, with a reason.

- **Default is deny.**
- A transfer to `cloud_model` additionally needs **per-transfer confirmation**. Before
  sending, the board lists exactly which cards would leave the device and why each is
  cleared. Nothing is sent until the user confirms.
- Private cards trigger a visible cloud warning in the context package preview. A card that
  is denied is shown as denied, never silently dropped.
- Stances are never disclosed beyond the screen.
- Context packages are selected card by card: the user can add and remove cards before
  handing over.

## 11. Decision card

Status: planned (M2+).

A decision card has exactly eleven sections, each non-empty:

| # | Field | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `situation` | what is the case |
| 2 | `trigger` | what makes a decision necessary now |
| 3 | `conditions` | the relevant conditions |
| 4 | `facts` | the known facts, as card references |
| 5 | `priorities` | what matters most |
| 6 | `action` | the chosen or preferred action |
| 7 | `alternatives` | the options not chosen |
| 8 | `exclusions` | conditions that rule an option out |
| 9 | `rationale` | why |
| 10 | `cardsUsed` | ids of the cards used. Every id must exist. |
| 11 | `outcome` | `open` until known |

A decision stores all eleven fields. Process and result are rated separately ("good
decision, bad outcome" is a valid verdict). When the outcome is entered, an experience card
is proposed, linked to the decision and to the cards used, and source statistics update.

## 12. Source profile

Status: planned (M2+).

A source has a **profile of separate dimensions**, never one credibility value. Every
dimension carries its sample size, and a dimension with fewer than five samples shows "too
little data" instead of a figure.

| Dimension | Question |
| --- | --- |
| `hitAccuracy` | how often did a resolved forecast come true, and how well calibrated |
| `reliability` | how stable are its statements over time |
| `topicCompetence` | hit accuracy per topic |
| `agreement` | how often it coincides with my stances (private, computed from my stances) |
| `deviation` | where it differs from me |
| `sourceQuality` | how often it evidences claims instead of merely asserting them |
| `historicalPerformance` | its track record over time |
| `counterPerspectiveValue` | how often it was right where I was wrong |

There is no "best source" ranking. Sorting is by a chosen dimension only. Every number links
to the cards behind it.

## 13. Further ideas from the second prototype

These ideas come from a second UX study and fit the canon.

- **Time slider** (planned (M2+)). Move the board to an earlier date and see it as it was,
  from real timestamps on archived versions, not from replaying a log.
- **What-if layer** (planned (M2+)). A non-persisting scenario layer with your own
  assumptions, savable and attachable to a decision. It never changes real cards.
- **World collision** (planned (M2+)). Two local worlds overlaid on one question: shared
  cards, diverging cards, and cards from one world that do not fit the other, with no
  consensus required.
- **Source pass** (planned (M1) for the counter, deeper chain planned (M2+)). A button on
  every card, seat, edge and decision showing the provenance chain and the counter
  "N findings · M origins".
- **Loop strip** (planned (M2+)). A thin strip showing the loop: experience, card, world,
  board, analysis, decision, action, outcome.

## 14. Review flow

Status: planned (M2+).

The review turns decisions into experience and should take under five minutes.

1. **What happened?** Free text and optional evidence cards.
2. **Resolve forecasts.** Each open forecast is marked came true, did not, partly, or left
   open (void is not counted in scores).
3. **Rate the decision.** Process and outcome separately, plus one lesson.
4. **Revisit stances.** The system shows the stance you held at the time next to the outcome.
   A stance change adds a version and never overwrites.
5. **Accept proposals.** An experience card, rule statistics and source-profile updates are
   proposed and accepted one by one or together.
