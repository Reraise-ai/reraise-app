> “With its MEAL study, the Federal Institute for Risk Assessment (BfR) examined 90% of the foods most consumed in Germany for various substances, including nickel. The result: the main food groups 'pulses, nuts, oilseeds and spices' and 'coffee, cocoa and tea' show the highest mean nickel contents, at about 1.6 milligrams per kilogram (mg/kg) and 1.5 mg/kg respectively.”

Quoted from BfR (Bundesinstitut für Risikobewertung), Nickel: Schätzung der langfristigen Aufnahme über Lebensmittel auf Grundlage der BfR-MEAL-Studie, Mitteilung Nr. 033/2022.

- context: The BfR carried out the MEAL study (the first German total diet study). Adults take in 1.4 µg/kg body weight/day on average (P50), high consumers (P95) 3.2 µg/kg body weight/day. Children take in 5.5 µg/kg body weight/day on average. The TDI is exceeded in only <5% of highly exposed children.
- upperLimit: no proposed maximum amount for addition to supplements, because nickel is not on the EU positive list of minerals for food supplements
- perDay: no maximum amount for nickel in food supplements; TDI adopted from EFSA: 13 µg/kg body weight/day
